# Day 3: Advanced SDLC Models (V-Model & Agile)

## 1. The V-Model (Verification and Validation Model)
* **Definition:** An extension of the Waterfall model where every development phase has a corresponding, parallel testing phase. The process steps bend upwards after coding to form a "V" shape.
* **How it Works (The "Shift-Left" Approach):** Testing does not wait until the end of the project. Test plans and test cases are created early during the **Verification** phases (analyzing requirements and design) and are executed later during the **Validation** phases (testing the compiled code). 
* **Pros:** Defects in requirements or design are found immediately, saving massive amounts of time and money compared to Waterfall.
* **Cons:** It remains a rigid model. If core business requirements change midway through the project, it is difficult and time-consuming to update all the corresponding documents and test plans.

## 2. The Agile Model
* **Definition:** An iterative and incremental approach to software development. Instead of building the entire product at once, the software is built and released in small, continuous cycles.
* **How it Works (Sprints):** The project is broken down into fast, 2-to-4-week development cycles called **Sprints**. At the end of every Sprint, a working, tested piece of the software is delivered to the client for immediate feedback.
* **The Role of QA in Agile:** Testing is continuous. QA Engineers test new features daily as they are built by developers, rather than waiting for a dedicated "testing phase" at the end of a six-month project.
* **Pros:** Highly adaptable. If the client changes their mind or the market shifts, the team simply adapts the requirements for the next Sprint. This is the industry standard for modern software companies.

## 3. Concept Check: Waterfall vs. Agile
* **Waterfall:** Build the entire house over a year, then ask the buyer if they like it. (High risk if they want a different layout).
* **Agile:** Build the foundation, get feedback. Build the walls, get feedback. Finish the rooms, get feedback. (Low risk, highly adaptable).
